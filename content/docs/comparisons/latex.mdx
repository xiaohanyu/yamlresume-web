title: YAMLResume VS LaTeX
---

For decades, [LaTeX](https://www.latex-project.org) has been the de facto way
for engineers, researchers, and academics to produce a professionally typeset
resume. Write a `.tex` file, compile it, and get a PDF with typography that
Word cannot match. A rich template ecosystem grew around this workflow—
[Awesome-CV](https://github.com/posquit0/Awesome-CV) (20,000+ GitHub stars),
[moderncv](https://github.com/moderncv/moderncv),
[Jake's Resume](https://github.com/jakegut/resume),
[Deedy-Resume](https://github.com/deedy/Deedy-Resume),
[AltaCV](https://github.com/liantze/AltaCV)—and
[Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/tagged/cv) hosts hundreds
of CV templates that compile in your browser.

From the official LaTeX project website:

```markdown
LaTeX is a document preparation system for high-quality typesetting. It is
most often used for medium-to-large technical or scientific documents but it
can be used for almost any form of publishing.
```

Here is the thing, though: **YAMLResume is not an alternative to LaTeX—it is
built on it.** YAMLResume's [LaTeX engine](/docs/layouts/latex) generates
`.tex` source from your YAML and compiles it with XeLaTeX, the same engine
that powers hand-written LaTeX resumes. The real comparison is between two
*authoring workflows*: writing your resume directly in TeX markup, or writing
it as structured YAML and letting a compiler generate the TeX for you.

Here is how the two workflows compare:

## Key Differences Explained

### Authoring: Content vs. Markup

The most immediate difference is what you look at all day. Here is the same
job entry, both ways.

In a hand-written Awesome-CV-style resume:

```latex
\cventry{Jan 2020 -- Present}
  {Senior Software Engineer}
  {ACME Inc.}
  {San Francisco, CA}
  {
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Led a team of 5 engineers building payments infrastructure
      \item Reduced p95 latency by 40\% via query optimization
    \end{itemize}
  }
```

In YAMLResume:

```yaml
work:
  - name: ACME Inc.
    position: Senior Software Engineer
    startDate: Jan 2020
    summary: |
      - Led a team of 5 engineers building payments infrastructure
      - Reduced p95 latency by 40% via query optimization
```

Both produce a professionally typeset entry. But in the LaTeX version, your
content is wrapped in macros, braces, and environments; in the YAML version,
your content *is* the file. Anyone—including a non-programmer colleague, a
recruiter, or your future self on a phone—can read and edit the YAML. And
because the markup is a compiler's job, you can change how every date in the
document is rendered by changing zero lines of your content.

### Validation and Debugging

This is where the two workflows diverge most sharply.

With hand-written LaTeX, your only safety net is the compiler. TeX errors are
famous for being cryptic and cascaded: a stray `&` in your job title can
surface as `Misplaced \omit` somewhere deep inside a class file, and tracking
it down requires reading the log carefully. Content errors—a malformed date,
a misspelled field—are just text to TeX; it will happily typeset `startDtae`.

YAMLResume validates your *data* before a single TeX line is emitted. The
[compiler](/docs/compiler/architecture) checks your resume against a strict
Zod schema and reports [clang-style
diagnostics](/blog/yamlresume-compiler) with line numbers, column numbers, and
the offending source:

```console
$ yamlresume validate my-resume.yml
my-resume.yml:9:12: warning: email is invalid.
    email: hi@pp
           ^
my-resume.yml:38:10: error: city is too short.
    city: S
          ^
```

By the time XeLaTeX runs, the data is known-good, and compile failures become
rare enough that they are actual news—usually a font or package issue that
`doctor` already flagged.

### Separation of Content and Presentation

Hand-written LaTeX entangles content and presentation. Every entry is wrapped
in template-specific macros, which means **switching templates means
rewriting your resume**. Moving from moderncv to AwesomeCV is not a theme
change; it is converting every `\cventry` into another macro (or whatever
the new class expects) and reshuffling arguments.

YAMLResume enforces [separation of
concerns](https://github.com/yamlresume/yamlresume) as a design principle:

- **`content`**: what your resume says
- **`locale`**: the language and region it says it in
- **`layouts`**: how it looks—engine, template, page, typography, section
  aliases and order

Want to see your resume in the [jake](/docs/layouts/latex/templates/jake)
style instead of [moderncv-banking](/docs/layouts/latex/templates/moderncv-banking)?
Change one line in `layouts`; the content file is untouched. This also makes
per-application tailoring trivial: keep one canonical content file, and check
in small layout variants that reorder or alias sections.

### One Source, Four Engines

A `.tex` file compiles to PDF. That is the whole pipeline, and for many
people it is enough—until a recruiter asks for a Word version, or you want
your resume as a page on your personal site, or you need a plain-text version
to paste into an ATS form. Pandoc can convert LaTeX to DOCX or HTML, but the
results are typographically mediocre compared to a purpose-built engine,
because the conversion has to guess intent from markup.

YAMLResume ships four first-class engines—[DOCX](/docs/layouts/docx),
[HTML](/docs/layouts/html), [Markdown](/docs/layouts/markdown), and
[LaTeX](/docs/layouts/latex)—and one `yamlresume build` produces all of them
from the same source. The [Markdown engine](/docs/layouts/markdown) is
particularly useful in 2026: it gives LLMs and ATS parsers clean, structured
text extracted from the same validated data.

### Internationalization

Writing a German or Chinese resume by hand in LaTeX is a project in itself.
German wants `babel` with `ngerman`, German date strings, and correct
quotation marks. Chinese requires choosing between ctex, xeCJK, or luatexja,
installing CJK fonts, and configuring font fallbacks—well-trodden paths for
TeX veterans, but genuinely days of effort the first time. Every additional
language multiplies the work, because nothing is shared between documents.

In YAMLResume you set the [`locale`](/docs/locales) key. The compiler translates
section headings, country names, degrees, skill levels, and fluency levels;
formats dates and addresses idiomatically; applies locale-appropriate
punctuation rules (including full-width punctuation for CJK and non-breaking
spaces before high punctuation in French); and configures
[babel](https://ctan.org/pkg/babel?lang=en) and the right CJK packages for
XeLaTeX automatically. And [`yamlresume ai translate`](/docs/ai/translate) can
produce a structure-preserving translation of your entire resume into any
supported locale—turning a multi-day typesetting project into a single command.

### The Customization Ceiling

To be fair: hand-written LaTeX wins the control contest, and it is not close.
Anything TeX can typeset, you can put in a hand-written resume—TikZ diagrams,
custom macros, precise micro-typographic tweaks, `biblatex` publication lists
for academic CVs, multi-page layouts, a cover letter sharing the same class.
If you are a TeX expert who enjoys this, YAMLResume will feel like guardrails.

But that ceiling comes with a cost: everything is your responsibility, forever.
Every macro conflict, package upgrade, and font edge case is yours to fix.
YAMLResume trades the infinite ceiling for a high floor: curated templates
encode professional typography decisions so you cannot make bad ones, while
still exposing the knobs that matter—[paper size, margins, and page
numbers](/docs/layouts/latex#page), [font family, size, and line
spacing](/docs/layouts/latex#typography), and [section aliases and
ordering](/docs/layouts/sections/aliases).

And if you ever hit the ceiling, there is an escape hatch: the generated
`.tex` source is emitted alongside your PDF. You can read it to learn, or
fork it and take over from there. YAMLResume does not hide LaTeX from you; it
just does not require you.

## Ecosystem

### Templates

The hand-written LaTeX ecosystem's greatest strength is its scale: thousands
of resume templates across GitHub, [Overleaf's
gallery](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/tagged/cv), CTAN, and
curation sites, in every visual style imaginable. The trade-off is quality
variance—templates range from meticulously maintained classes to one-off
exports with hardcoded values, outdated package usage, and no validation.

YAMLResume takes the curated approach: a small set of official
[LaTeX](/docs/layouts/latex/templates) templates (moderncv-classic,
moderncv-casual, moderncv-banking, jake, etc.), each tested against all 12
locale codes, so a template switch never silently breaks a localized resume.

### Overleaf

[Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com) is the closest thing the LaTeX world has
to a YAMLResume-like experience: zero installation, a template gallery, and
auto-compile on every keystroke, plus genuinely excellent real-time
collaboration. If you want to stay in a LaTeX workflow, it is the easiest way
to do it.

The trade-offs are structural: your project lives in a cloud service;
features like version history, track changes, and GitHub synchronization sit
behind paid plans; and you still have a `.tex` document—no structured data, no
multi-format export, no schema validation, no CLI. YAMLResume gives you
Overleaf-style iteration speed locally through [`yamlresume
dev`](/blog/dev-mode), with Git as your version history.

### CI/CD

Both workflows automate. The LaTeX world has community solutions like the
[latex-action](https://github.com/xu-cheng/latex-action) for GitHub Actions, and
Overleaf can sync with GitHub as a paid feature. YAMLResume ships an [official
GitHub Action](https://github.com/yamlresume/action) that validates your resume
and builds all four formats in one step:

```yaml
- uses: yamlresume/action@v0.16.1
  with:
    resumes: |
      resume-en.yml
      resume-zh.yml
      resume-fr.yml
```

Because the input is structured YAML, CI can also do things no `.tex`
pipeline can: fail a pull request that introduces a schema error, diff
content changes without markup noise, or regenerate all localized variants
from a single edit.

## Summary

**Hand-written LaTeX** excels if you want:

- Total typographic control—custom macros, TikZ graphics, `biblatex`
  bibliographies, precise spacing, any layout you can imagine
- Access to thousands of community templates and classes in every style
- The same toolchain you already use for papers, theses, and slides
- A genuinely fun craft, if TeX is a skill you enjoy maintaining
- Cloud convenience and collaboration through Overleaf

**YAMLResume** excels if you want:

- LaTeX-grade PDF typography—via XeLaTeX—without writing or debugging TeX
- Structured, validated data with clang-style errors instead of compile logs
- Extended output formats (DOCX, HTML, Markdown, LaTeX/PDF) from one source file
- Deep [internationalization](/docs/locales) across 10 languages and 12 locale
  codes, including automatic CJK setup, with AI-assisted translation
- Template switching without rewriting content, thanks to strict separation
  of content, locale, and layouts
- A developer toolchain: watch mode, environment diagnostics, Docker,
  Homebrew, and a GitHub Action for CI/CD

Choose **hand-written LaTeX** if you already know TeX, need layouts beyond
what any structured schema can express, or consider typesetting part of the
craft.

Choose **YAMLResume** if you want the output LaTeX is famous for, but would
rather your resume read like data than like a program.
