title: YAMLResume VS Microsoft Word
---

[Microsoft Word](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/word) is how
most of the world writes resumes. It is the default format for job
applications, it is installed on hundreds of millions of computers, and its
template gallery offers thousands of designs. If a recruiter asks you to "send
over your resume," nine times out of ten they expect a `.docx` or a PDF
exported from Word.

From Microsoft's own product page:

```markdown
- Word works across devices—on desktop (Windows and macOS), mobile, tablets
  (iOS and Android) or online.
- Word for the web is free for anyone to use online.
- Real-time collaboration means fewer email attachments and faster feedback.
```

The important thing is this: **choosing YAMLResume does not mean giving up
Word-compatible output.** YAMLResume has a dedicated [DOCX engine](/docs/layouts/docx)
that builds Microsoft Word documents from your YAML source with one command.
The real comparison is not "YAML versus `.docx`"—it is **manual document
editing versus compiled, structured output**. That difference touches
version control, validation, consistency, multi-format publishing, and
automation.

Here is how the two workflows compare:

## Key Differences Explained

### Manual Formatting vs. Generated Formatting

In Word, formatting is your job. Every bullet indent, every date tab stop, and
every bold job title is a decision you make and keep remaking. Over months of
edits, documents drift: spacing becomes inconsistent, a new entry uses a
slightly different bullet, and "one tiny change" costs twenty minutes of
fiddling. Studies show most Word users never touch named styles, so formatting
ends up as one-off inline tweaks that are painful to revise.

In YAMLResume, formatting is the compiler's job. Your content is data; the
[DOCX engine](/docs/layouts/docx) and the [LaTeX engine](/docs/layouts/latex)
render it through templates that enforce consistency. Change your job title in
one place and every format—DOCX, PDF, HTML, Markdown—picks it up on the next
build. The template makes each design decision once, and gets it right.

### Plain Text vs. Binary

A `.docx` file is a ZIP archive full of XML. Git cannot diff it in a useful way,
merging two versions of a resume means copy-pasting by hand, and "what changed
between March and June?" means opening both files and comparing them side by
side. This is why Word resumes pile up as `Resume.docx`, `Resume_new.docx`,
`Resume_newer.docx`, `Resume_FINAL_tracked.docx` in a folder nobody dares to
clean up.

A YAMLResume file is plain text. `git diff` shows exactly what changed:

```diff
-    position: Senior Software Engineer
+    position: Staff Software Engineer
     startDate: Jan 2020
-    endDate: Dec 2024
+    endDate:
```

Plain text opens up the usual developer tools: branches per job application,
pull-request reviews of your own career history, automated builds in CI, and a
permanent, searchable archive of every resume you have ever had.

### Validation: Spellcheck Is Not Structure Checking

Word will underline a misspelled employer, but it will let you save a resume
where the end date comes before the start date, the email is missing an `@`,
or the city field is a single letter. None of those are spelling errors, so
none of them get flagged.

YAMLResume validates structure before rendering anything. The
[compiler](/docs/compiler/architecture) checks your resume against a strict
Zod schema and reports [clang-style
diagnostics](/blog/yamlresume-compiler) with line numbers and source snippets,
so errors are caught while you write, not when a recruiter notices them.

### Tailoring Without the Copy Chaos

Career advice is clear: tailor your resume for each application. In Word, that
usually means choosing Save As and editing a copy. But the copies quickly fall
out of sync—fix a typo in one file, forget the other, and now you have two
different resumes and no idea which is right.

In YAMLResume, your content file stays the single source of truth. Tailoring
happens in layout configs: [section aliases and ordering](/docs/layouts/sections/aliases)
can change per variant, and Git tracks them right next to your content. Ten
tailored variants are just ten small layout files pointing to the same
resume—not ten separate documents drifting apart.

### Internationalization, Fairly Stated

Word is good at this: it handles multilingual documents, spellchecking, and
complex scripts—including Chinese, Japanese, and Korean—very well, and it is
the standard tool for resumes in many non-English countries. If you write one
resume in one language, Word's multilingual support is not your problem.

YAMLResume shines when one career must speak several languages. Setting the
`locale` key translates section headings, degrees, skill levels, and country
names; formats dates and addresses in local style; and applies locale
punctuation rules across [10 languages and 12 locale
codes](/docs/locale). Combined with [`yamlresume ai
translate`](/docs/ai/translate), an English-to-Japanese variant is one command
away, generated from the same source so the two never drift. In Word, that
means two documents and a translator.

### ATS and Document Structure

Applicant tracking systems parse `.docx` files well—when the document is
simple. The problem is that most Word resume templates are *not* simple:
two-column layouts, tables, text boxes, icons, and graphics are exactly the
things that confuse parsers, and a template gallery cannot tell you which
designs are safe. The result is a resume that looks beautiful in Word but
turns into gibberish in the ATS.

YAMLResume's DOCX output is generated from structured data, so the underlying
document is clean and predictable. And when you need text you can reliably
extract, the [Markdown engine](/docs/layouts/markdown) emits the same resume as
pure structured text that any parser—or LLM—reads perfectly.

## Ecosystem

### Templates

Microsoft's template ecosystem is enormous: the
[Microsoft Create](https://create.microsoft.com/en-us/templates/resumes)
gallery and countless third-party sites offer thousands of Word resume
templates, free and paid, in every visual style. The downside is uneven
quality—for every well-built template there are dozens built with tables and
text boxes that make editing hard and break ATS parsers.

YAMLResume ships a small curated set: the [calm](/docs/layouts/docx/templates/calm)
template for DOCX output, plus [LaTeX](/docs/layouts/latex/templates) and
[HTML](/docs/layouts/html/templates) templates, all validated against every
supported locale. Because templates are engine settings rather than documents,
switching designs never means rewriting content.

### Recruiters and the `.docx` Format

Many recruiters and application systems specifically request Word documents—
some ATS setups parse `.docx` more reliably than PDF, and agencies often
remove your contact details before forwarding, which is common even if you
dislike it. This is exactly why YAMLResume includes a built-in DOCX engine.
The [calm](/docs/layouts/docx/templates/calm) template produces a clean Word
document from the same YAML source, with [page](/docs/layouts/docx#page),
[typography](/docs/layouts/docx#typography), and advanced options (clickable
URLs, contact icons) that match the other engines.

One caveat applies to any DOCX file, generated or not: fonts must exist on the
machine where the document is opened, or Word substitutes them. Choose widely
available fonts for your DOCX layout, and keep the LaTeX/PDF engine as your
reference for perfect-looking output.

### Automation

Word has macros; YAMLResume has a pipeline. The [GitHub
Action](https://github.com/yamlresume/action) builds every format on every
push, the [Docker image](/docs/contributing/docker) builds anywhere without
local setup, and [`@yamlresume/node`](/docs/ecosystem/node) lets applications
read, validate, and build resumes programmatically. For a document you update
once a year this is overkill; for a living resume that evolves with your
career—and for teams maintaining resumes at scale—it changes how you maintain
resumes.

## Summary

**Microsoft Word** excels if you want:

- A format that works everywhere—every recruiter can open and edit a `.docx`
- A WYSIWYG editor with zero setup and no build step
- Great collaboration: real-time co-authoring, comments, track changes
- A huge template gallery covering every industry and visual style
- A familiar tool you already own and use for everything else

**YAMLResume** excels if you want:

- One plain-text source file in Git, with real diffs, history, and branches
- Generated, consistent formatting across [DOCX](/docs/layouts/docx),
  [PDF](/docs/layouts/latex), [HTML](/docs/layouts/html), and
  [Markdown](/docs/layouts/markdown)
- [Schema validation](/docs/compiler/schema) that catches [structural
  errors](/docs/cli#validate) before recruiters do
- Deep [internationalization](/docs/locale): [10 languages across 12 locale
  codes](/docs/locale) with [AI translation](/docs/ai/translate), from a single
  source of truth
- ATS-safe output by design, plus a [Markdown engine](/docs/layouts/markdown)
  for text pipelines
- Automation: [watch mode](/blog/dev-mode), [CI/CD
  builds](/docs/ecosystem/action), [Docker](/docs/contributing/docker), and a
  [Node.js API](/docs/ecosystem/node)
- Word compatibility *without* the Word workflow, via the [built-in DOCX
  engine](/docs/layouts/docx)

Choose **Microsoft Word** if you write one resume a year, collaborate with
non-technical people who live in Office, or simply value immediacy over
structure.

Choose **YAMLResume** if your resume is a living document—versioned,
tailored per application, published in multiple formats and languages, and
maintained like the rest of your work: as code. And when a recruiter asks for
a Word version, `yamlresume build` has you covered.
